Validation Experiments & Hypotheses

This section outlines testable hypotheses for BenchmarkHub's core assumptions and designs lean experiments to validate them. By running these in an 8-week sprint, we de-risk the build by confirming problem resonance, solution fit, pricing viability, and acquisition channels before full development. Focus is on AI engineers as primary users, leveraging low-cost methods like interviews and prototypes.

1. Hypothesis Framework

Hypotheses are structured to test critical risks across problem, solution, pricing, and channel dimensions. We prioritize 10 hypotheses, with 3 critical ones marked 🔴.

Hypothesis #1: Problem Existence (Custom Benchmark Need) 🔴 Critical

Statement: We believe that AI engineers at tech companies will actively seek tools for task-specific LLM evaluation if they are deploying models for production use cases like summarization or code generation. We will know this is true when we see 65%+ of surveyed engineers confirm this as a top-3 pain point AND 4%+ landing page interest rate.

Risk Level: 🔴 Critical (product fails if real-world evaluation isn't a priority).

Current Evidence:
Supporting: Industry reports (e.g., Gartner predicts 80% of AI projects fail due to poor model selection); Reddit/Hacker News threads show frustration with academic benchmarks.
Contradicting: None identified.
Gaps: Limited direct interviews with enterprise users.

Experiment Design:
Method: Targeted surveys + landing page test.
Sample Size: 25 surveys, 800 visitors.
Duration: 2 weeks.
Cost: $400 (LinkedIn ads) + 15 hours (outreach).

MetricFailMinimumSuccessHome Run
Pain point confirmation<40%40-65%65-85%>85%
Landing page interest<2%2-4%4-8%>8%

Next Steps if Validated: Advance to solution testing.
Next Steps if Invalidated: Explore adjacent pains like prompt engineering tools.

Hypothesis #2: Problem Existence (Sharing & Community Need)

Statement: We believe that AI researchers and enthusiasts will share benchmark results publicly if they lack easy ways to collaborate on evaluations. We will know this is true when we see 50%+ of interviewees express interest in a public library AND 3%+ community signup rate.

Risk Level: 🟡 High (weakens network effects).

Current Evidence:
Supporting: Growth of platforms like Hugging Face shows demand for shared AI resources.
Contradicting: Some prefer private evals.
Gaps: No data on sharing frequency.

Experiment Design:

Cost: $200 (Twitter ads) + 10 hours.

Next Steps if Validated: Build community features early.

Hypothesis #3: Solution Fit (Ease of Custom Benchmarks) 🔴 Critical

Statement: We believe that AI engineers will use a no-code benchmark builder over manual scripting if we provide templates and unified API access for 50+ models. We will know this is true when we see 75%+ of prototype users complete a benchmark in <30 minutes AND rate it 7/10+ for usability.

Gaps: No hands-on testing yet.

2. Experiment Catalog

12 lean experiments to test the hypotheses, prioritized for quick iteration. Each includes method, setup, metrics, timeline, cost, and criteria.

Experiment #1: Problem Discovery Interviews (Tests #1, #2)

Method: Semi-structured Zoom interviews with AI engineers.

Setup:1. Recruit via LinkedIn/Reddit (r/MachineLearning). 2. $50 Amazon gift incentive. 3. 30-min script on eval pains. 4. Transcribe with Otter.ai.

Metrics: % confirming task-specific need; current manual time spend; alternative usage.

Timeline: 2 weeks.

Cost: $1,250 (25 incentives).

Success Criteria: ✅ Pass: 65%+ pain confirmation | ⚠️ Re-evaluate: 40-65% | ❌ Fail: <40%.

Owner: Founder/Product Lead.

Experiment #2: Landing Page Smoke Test (Tests #1, #3)

Method: Carrd page with benchmark demo and signup.

Setup:1. Headlines: A: "Benchmark LLMs for Your Tasks in Minutes" | B: "End Model Selection Guesswork". 2. Drive 800 visitors via Google Ads. 3. Track with GA4.

Metrics: Signup rate; variant performance; bounce rate.

Timeline: 2 weeks.

Cost: $400.

Success Criteria: ✅ >4% signup | ⚠️ 2-4% | ❌ <2%.

Owner: Marketing Lead.

Experiment #3: Wizard of Oz MVP (Tests #3, #4, #6)

Method: Manually run benchmarks using OpenRouter APIs + Google Sheets analysis.

Setup:1. Accept tasks via Typeform. 2. Execute on 5 models. 3. Deliver PDF report. 4. Survey satisfaction and pay willingness.

Metrics: Delivery time (<2 hours); NPS; % upgrade intent.

Timeline: 4 weeks (15 users).

Cost: $500 (API + incentives).

Success Criteria: ✅ 7.5/10+ satisfaction, 50%+ pay | ⚠️ 5-7.5/10 | ❌ <5/10.

Owner: Tech Lead.

Experiment #4: Pricing Survey (Van Westendorp) (Tests #6, #7)

Method: Survey on price perceptions for features.

Setup:1. 4 questions on too cheap/expensive. 2. Distribute via AI newsletters. 3. Target 100 responses.

Metrics: Optimal price point; % willing at $29.

Timeline: 1 week.

Cost: $200.

Success Criteria: ✅ $25-35 sweet spot | ⚠️ $15-25 | ❌ <$15.

Owner: Product Lead.

Experiment #5: Competitor Tear-Down Interviews (Tests #2, #3)

Method: Ask users about PromptFoo/HELM usage.

Setup:1. 10 interviews. 2. Probe gaps (e.g., custom tasks).

Metrics: % dissatisfaction; desired improvements.

Timeline: 1.5 weeks.

Cost: $500.

Success Criteria: ✅ 70%+ cite customization gaps | ⚠️ 40-70% | ❌ <40%.

Owner: Founder.

Experiment #6: Pre-Order Test (Tests #6)

Method: Gumroad page for early access at $29.

Setup:1. Promote to 500 targeted users. 2. Refund if not built.

Metrics: Orders; refund requests.

Timeline: 2 weeks.

Cost: $100 (fees).

Success Criteria: ✅ 10+ orders | ⚠️ 5-10 | ❌ <5.

Owner: Sales Lead.

Experiment #7: Fake Door Feature Test (Tests #4, #5)

Method: Landing page buttons for "CI/CD Integration" and "Team Workspace".

Setup:1. Track clicks. 2. 400 visitors via ads.

Metrics: Click rate per feature.

Timeline: 1 week.

Cost: $300.

Success Criteria: ✅ >10% clicks on key features | ⚠️ 5-10% | ❌ <5%.

Owner: Product Lead.

Experiment #8: Channel Testing (Tests #9)

Method: $100 ads each on Reddit, Twitter, LinkedIn.

Setup:1. Same creative. 2. Measure CAC to signup.

Metrics: Cost per signup; quality (interview follow-up).

Timeline: 2 weeks.

Cost: $300.

Success Criteria: ✅ Avg CAC <$12 | ⚠️ $12-20 | ❌ >$20.

Owner: Marketing.

Experiment #9: Referral Mechanism Test (Tests #5, #10)

Method: Add share button to Wizard of Oz reports.

Setup:1. Offer free credit for referrals. 2. Track 10 users.

Metrics: Viral coefficient (referrals/user).

Timeline: 2 weeks.

Cost: $100 (credits).

Success Criteria: ✅ >0.5 coefficient | ⚠️ 0.1-0.5 | ❌ <0.1.

Owner: Growth Lead.

Experiment #10: Retention Experiment (Tests #4)

Method: Follow-up email to Wizard users for second benchmark.

Setup:1. Offer discount. 2. 15 users.

Metrics: Repeat rate; feedback on stickiness.

Timeline: 3 weeks.

Cost: $200.

Success Criteria: ✅ 40%+ repeat | ⚠️ 20-40% | ❌ <20%.

Owner: Product.

Experiment #11: Community Mockup Test (Tests #2, #5)

Method: Figma prototype of library; user walkthrough.

Setup:1. 12 users browse/fork sim. 2. Measure engagement time.

Metrics: Session time; fork intent.

Timeline: 2 weeks.

Cost: $150.

Success Criteria: ✅ >5 min avg time | ⚠️ 2-5 min | ❌ <2 min.

Owner: UX Designer.

Experiment #12: Influencer Pilot (Tests #10)

Method: Co-benchmark with 2 AI YouTubers.

Setup:1. Provide WoZ access. 2. Track referral links.

Metrics: Traffic; signups from video.

Timeline: 3 weeks.

Cost: $400.

Success Criteria: ✅ 200+ visits | ⚠️ 100-200 | ❌ <100.

6. Pivot Triggers & Contingency Plans

Trigger #1: Problem Doesn't Exist

Signal: <50% confirm eval pains (#1).
Action: Broader surveys on AI workflow pains; interview alternatives.
Pivot Options: Shift to prompt optimization tool or general MLOps dashboard.

Trigger #2: Solution Doesn't Resonate

Signal: <60% satisfaction (#3).
Action: Usability heatmaps; A/B templates.
Pivot Options: CLI-only tool; focus on analysis, not building.

Trigger #3: Won't Pay Enough

Signal: Optimal price <$20 (#4,6).
Action: Cost breakdown; value prop tweaks.
Pivot Options: Open-source core, monetize add-ons; enterprise-only.

Trigger #4: Can't Acquire Efficiently

Signal: CAC >$15 all channels (#8,9).
Action: SEO content audit; community seeding.
Pivot Options: Partner with Hugging Face; product-led via GitHub integration.
