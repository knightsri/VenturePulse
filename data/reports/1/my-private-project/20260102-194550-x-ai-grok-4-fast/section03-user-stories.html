Section 03: User Stories & Problem Scenarios

This section outlines key user personas, their daily challenges in evaluating LLMs, and how BenchmarkHub addresses them. It includes vivid scenarios, prioritized user stories, jobs-to-be-done, validation evidence, journey frictions, and transformed experiences post-solution.

Primary User Personas

👨‍💻 Persona #1: Alex the AI Engineer

Demographics:

  • Age Range: 28-35 years old
  • Location: Urban (San Francisco Bay Area)
  • Occupation: Senior AI Engineer at a mid-sized tech startup (50-200 employees)
  • Income Level: $120K-$160K
  • Tech Savviness: High
  • Decision-Making Authority: Team influencer / Budget owner for tools

Background Story: Alex graduated with a CS degree and has 7 years in software engineering, pivoting to AI two years ago. At his startup building customer support chatbots, he spends 60% of his time evaluating LLMs for tasks like intent classification. His days involve coding prototypes, but model selection bottlenecks slow deployments. He aims to lead AI initiatives and dreams of scaling the company to unicorn status. Success means deploying reliable AI that reduces support tickets by 50% without constant retraining.

Current Pain Points:

  1. Pain #1: Academic benchmarks like MMLU don't match real chatbot performance; weekly evaluations waste 10+ hours, leading to deployment delays and frustration.
  2. Pain #2: Running custom tests costs $50-200 in API fees per cycle; time spent scripting evaluations adds 20 hours/month.
  3. Pain #3: Model updates (e.g., GPT-4o) invalidate prior tests; he rebuilds from scratch, causing missed deadlines.
  4. Pain #4: No easy way to share results with non-technical stakeholders; explanations take extra meetings.
  5. Pain #5: Comparing 10+ models for latency/cost/accuracy is manual; spreadsheets become outdated quickly.
  6. Pain #6: Team collaboration on benchmarks is chaotic via email/Slack; versions get lost.
  7. Pain #7: Biased provider claims lead to poor choices; a wrong pick once cost the team a week of rework.

Goals & Desired Outcomes:

  • Primary Goal: Quickly identify the best LLM for specific production tasks like summarization.
  • Secondary Goals: Track model performance over time; collaborate with team; optimize for cost under $0.01 per query.
  • Emotional Outcome: Confident and empowered, not second-guessing choices.
  • Success Metrics: 80% accuracy in task-specific evals; benchmarks run in under 1 hour; ROI via reduced API spend.

Current Solutions & Alternatives: Uses PromptFoo for local testing (CLI-only, no sharing); lmsys leaderboard for quick checks (academic focus, not customizable). Abandoned manual Google Colab scripts due to scalability issues. Spends $100/month on API keys + 15 hours/week on workarounds. Failures: Lack of community insights and easy forking.

Buying Behavior:

  • Trigger: New project kickoff or model release announcement.
  • Research Process: Reddit (r/MachineLearning), Twitter, G2 reviews.
  • Decision Criteria: Ease of use, integration with APIs, community size.
  • Budget: $50-200/month for Pro tools.
  • Adoption Barriers: Learning curve; must integrate with existing MLOps (e.g., GitHub).

Her routine includes grant writing, experiments, and teaching. Goals: Advance academia while consulting; success is peer-reviewed papers with reproducible benchmarks that influence industry standards.

Current Pain Points:

  1. Pain #1: Creating reproducible benchmarks for niche tasks like bias detection takes days; no standard templates.
  2. Pain #2: API costs strain grants; $200+ per experiment limits iterations.
  3. Pain #3: Sharing results for peer review via PDFs loses interactivity; collaborators request raw data.
  4. Pain #4: Model evals aren't version-controlled; updates break comparisons.
  5. Pain #5: Community benchmarks are scarce for academic tasks; she reinvents wheels.
  6. Pain #6: Manual failure analysis (e.g., hallucination patterns) is tedious.
  7. Pain #7: No easy way to cite or fork others' work ethically.

Goals & Desired Outcomes:

  • Primary Goal: Build and share reproducible benchmarks for research validity.
  • Secondary Goals: Analyze failure modes; track historical data; collaborate globally.
  • Emotional Outcome: Credible and connected to the community.
  • Success Metrics: Benchmarks cited in 5+ papers/year; evals complete in 2 hours.

Current Solutions & Alternatives: HELM for standardized evals (too rigid for custom research); Jupyter notebooks shared on GitHub (messy for non-coders). Tried OpenAI Evals but it's provider-specific. Spends $50/month on compute + 10 hours/week. Failures: Lack of community forking and stats tools.

Buying Behavior:

  • Trigger: Paper deadline or conference call for proposals.
  • Research Process: ArXiv, academic forums, Hugging Face discussions.
  • Decision Criteria: Reproducibility, free tier, export options.
  • Budget: $0-50/month (prefers free for grants).
  • Adoption Barriers: Must be open-source friendly; no paywalls for sharing.

"Day in the Life" Scenarios

📅 Scenario #1: Model Selection Sprint for Deadline

Context:

  • Who: Alex the AI Engineer
  • When: Tuesday, 9 AM, weekly during sprints
  • Where: Office desk
  • What: Evaluate LLMs for chatbot intent detection before Friday demo

Current Experience (Before Solution):

Alex starts his Tuesday at 9 AM, staring at a Jira ticket: "Select LLM for intent classifier – accuracy >85%, latency <2s." He pulls up lmsys.org for quick scores but sighs—it's all trivia questions, not real intents like "refund request." He switches to his laptop, firing up a Colab notebook with 50 test cases he's curated over months. Copy-pasting prompts into OpenAI Playground for GPT-4, then Anthropic for Claude—each run takes 5 minutes, and he tracks results in a Google Sheet. By 11 AM, he's tested three models, but costs are mounting ($15 already), and Claude hallucinates on edge cases. Lunch break hits; he resumes at 1 PM, adding Hugging Face models via API, but setup glitches eat 30 minutes. Latency tests require custom timers—another hour. At 4 PM, the sheet has partial data: GPT-4 leads, but no stats or visuals. He emails the team messy charts, but the PM wants confidence intervals. By 6 PM, 8 hours in, he's only covered 60% of models, frustrated and doubting picks. Outcome: Partial recommendation, sprint delayed, team morale dips.

Pain Points Highlighted:

  • Fragmented tools (Colab + Sheets + APIs)
  • Time wasted: 8+ hours for incomplete results
  • Emotional: Overwhelmed, anxious about deadlines
  • Outcome: Delayed deployment, $20+ wasted costs

📅 Scenario #2: Research Benchmark Build for Paper

Context:

  • Who: Dr. Elena the AI Researcher
  • When: Thursday afternoon, monthly for publications
  • Where: Home office
  • What: Create bias benchmark for medical text summarization

Current Experience (Before Solution):

Elena settles in Thursday at 2 PM, deadline looming for a NeurIPS submission. She needs a benchmark for 100 medical abstracts, checking for gender bias in summaries. No templates exist, so she drafts prompts in a Word doc, then codes an eval script in Python using NLTK for bias metrics—two hours just for setup. Uploading cases to a local server, she runs Llama via Hugging Face, but GPU limits force sequential execution; each model takes 20 minutes. By 5 PM, two models done, but Claude via API crashes on batch size. She tweaks code, losing an hour. Costs hit $30 for cloud compute. At 7 PM, raw outputs pile up; manual scoring for "judge" evals adds three hours. No easy stats— she installs SciPy, plots in Matplotlib. Sharing with co-author via GitHub is messy; they request reruns. By 10 PM, 7 hours later, benchmark is half-built, biased results unanalyzed. She skips dinner, stressed about reproducibility. Outcome: Incomplete paper draft, grant time justification questioned.

Pain Points Highlighted:

  • Custom scripting overhead
  • Time wasted: 7+ hours for partial benchmark
  • Emotional: Stressed, isolated in process
  • Outcome: Delayed publication, $30+ costs
